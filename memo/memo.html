After duty-free Europe: where Bangladeshi apparel should compete next

For exporters, lenders, policy teamsFrom Md. ZubayerDate 15 Nov 2026Data 2015 to 2025; EU to Sep 2026
The question, and the answer

Bangladesh was due to leave the UN's least developed country list on 24 November 2026. On 29 October the UN General Assembly moved that to 24 November 2029. With the EU's three-year transition, duty-free access to Europe now runs to about November 2032. The deferral buys time; it does not change the destination.

My answer: defend knitwear in the EU by making it GSP+ ready, take woven growth to the US and UK, and build man-made-fibre capacity for the categories China is leaving. Waiting for another extension is not a plan: Bangladesh is already losing EU share in 2026, before any tariff has changed.

Recommendation: three bets

1 · Defend

EU knitwear, GSP+ ready

Knitwear is EUR 11.8bn of Bangladesh's EUR 19.5bn EU sales. It uses 80 to 90% local fabric, so most of it can pass GSP+'s two-stage origin rule. GSP+ would cut the duty bill from EUR 2.3bn to EUR 0.8bn.

2 · Redirect

Woven to the US and UK

Woven garments import about 60% of their fabric, so they fail GSP+ origin. The US has no preference to lose, and the UK now accepts imported fabric. Woven trousers are the biggest US category ($18.4bn); Bangladesh holds 18.3%.

3 · Build

Synthetic categories China is leaving

China's US share fell from 42% to 16% in outerwear and 38% to 18% in underwear. Vietnam took most of it; Bangladesh holds 10% and 9%. Winning more needs man-made-fibre fabric capacity.

What I am asking for

  1. Treat full EU duty as the base case from 2033 and GSP+ as upside. Price every new EU contract that runs past 2032 with the duty in it.
  2. Fund the GSP+ file now: origin evidence for knitwear, and the labour and environmental conventions the EU will check.
  3. Shift new woven capacity toward US and UK buyers, and pilot synthetic-fibre lines in outerwear and sportswear within 18 months.

What the data says

1. Bangladesh gained share for a decade, and the gains have stalled in Europe

From 2015 to 2022 Bangladesh took share in both big markets as China lost it. Since 2022 the two markets have split. In the US, China kept falling (36% to 15%) and Bangladesh kept rising (6.1% to 10.0%), though Vietnam took the largest slice. In the EU, China came back, from 28% in 2023 to 33% in 2025, and Bangladesh slipped from 22.1% to 20.4%. A reasonable reading is that Chinese goods priced out of the US are being sold into Europe.

2. Bangladesh wins on price, and the price has hardly moved

In the US, Bangladesh's price per item is below Vietnam's in every main group: 59 for T-shirts and 71 for woven trousers, with Vietnam at 100. Over ten years its T-shirt price moved from $1.59 to $1.66, while Vietnam's went from $2.58 to $2.80. The EU shows the same pattern in euros per kilogram. Cheapness is the moat. It is also the weakness: on margins this thin, a 12% duty cannot be absorbed, and buyers already move orders over price gaps of 1 to 2%.

3. The 2026 fall is not only currency

Bangladesh's EU sales fell 11.9% from January to September 2026, against a 2.5% fall for all suppliers. A stronger euro lowers the euro value of dollar-priced orders, but it affects Vietnam and China too, and both grew. Bangladesh's share of EU imports fell from 21.1% to 19.1% over those months. The usual explanations are higher wage and energy costs since 2024 and Chinese price competition; the trade data can show the loss, not its cause.

Tariff exposure and scenarios

4. What losing EU access would cost

Almost every EU apparel line carries a 12% MFN duty. Bangladesh pays nothing today under Everything But Arms. After 2032 its main EU competitors will also pay nothing: Vietnam under its free trade agreement from 2027, and India once its new agreement takes effect, targeted for 2027. China keeps paying 12%. So the question is not whether Bangladesh pays duty, but whether it pays duty while Vietnam and India do not.

5. GSP+ is worth fighting for, but it is not the base case

Two features of the new EU GSP rules, which apply from January 2027, make GSP+ hard to use for Bangladeshi apparel. First, the apparel safeguard can suspend the preference when one country's share of GSP apparel imports passes a threshold, and Bangladesh is far above it. Second, GSP+ needs two-stage origin, fabric and garment both made locally, where EBA needs one. Knitwear clears the second test; woven mostly does not. The government is pursuing GSP+ and an EU trade agreement in parallel. The sensible plan is to fight for GSP+ while pricing for full duty.

Other markets are kinder. The UK will keep apparel duty-free after graduation under its Enhanced Preferences tier, and in 2026 it relaxed origin rules so imported fabric qualifies. Bangladesh's UK share already rose from 14.6% in 2019 to 22.2% in 2025. In the US, Bangladesh pays normal duty plus a 10% Section 301 tariff since July 2026, the same footing as most rivals, with US policy still moving.

What would change my mind

  • An EU trade deal with Bangladesh would keep access and make Bet 1 a cost question, not a survival one.
  • US tariffs on Bangladesh well above Vietnam's would weaken Bets 2 and 3.
  • The EU-India agreement slipping well past 2027 would ease pressure in woven garments.
  • Bangladesh's EU share recovering in 2027 would suggest 2026 was timing and currency, not lost orders.

What this analysis cannot tell you

  • Margins and costs. Trade data shows prices and volumes, not factory profit. The duty-versus-margin argument relies on industry reporting.
  • Price per item mixes price and product. A lower unit value can mean a cheaper price or a simpler garment.
  • About 4.5% of 2025 EU imports, mostly EUR 4bn from China, has no product detail, so product-level EU shares understate China slightly.
  • The volume range is a model. Buyers also weigh lead time, compliance and relationships, not only duty.
